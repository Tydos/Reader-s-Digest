THINKING MACHINES LAB RELEASES INKLING (July 15–16)
Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling, its first model trained from
scratch and the largest U.S. open-weights model to date. Key specifications:

  - 975 billion total parameters (MoE architecture), 41 billion active
  - Multimodal: native text, image, and audio inputs; 1 million token context
  - Trained on 45 trillion tokens across text, images, audio, and video
  - Apache 2.0 license; weights available on Hugging Face (BF16 and NVFP4 variants)
  - API access via Tinker, TogetherAI, Fireworks, Modal, Databricks, and Baseten
  - Day-zero support in transformers, vLLM, SGLang, and llama.cpp

Benchmark highlights include 97.1% on AIME 2026, 77.6% on SWE-bench Verified,
79.8% on IFBench, and 78.0% on FORTRESS Adversarial — leading the open-weights
group on adversarial robustness. Inkling debuts at 41 on the Artificial Analysis
Intelligence Index, surpassing the previous leading U.S. open model Nemotron 3
Ultra (38). A smaller variant, Inkling-Small (276B params, 12B active), is in
preview with weights expected after testing completes.

The release positions Thinking Machines against closed frontier models from OpenAI,
Anthropic, and Google, with CEO Satya Nadella and Hugging Face's Clem Delangue both
recently arguing that enterprises using proprietary models pay twice — in
subscriptions and by surrendering domain knowledge embedded in prompts.

OPENAI DETAILS GPT-RED (July 15)
OpenAI published details on GPT-Red, an internal automated red-teaming model
trained via reinforcement learning to discover prompt injection vulnerabilities
and other attack vectors. Key findings:

  - GPT-Red succeeds on 84% of attack scenarios vs. 13% for human red-teamers
  - Used in training since GPT-5.3; directly incorporated into GPT-5.6 training
  - GPT-5.6 shows 6x fewer failures on direct prompt injection benchmarks
  - "Fake chain-of-thought" attacks that worked >95% against GPT-5.1 now succeed
    less than 10% against GPT-5.6
  - Tested against real-world targets including Vendy, an office vending machine AI

OpenAI will not release GPT-Red publicly, citing the danger of distributing
attack capabilities. The company positions this as a "safety flywheel" where
current models harden future releases. Gaps remain in multi-turn conversational
attacks and image-based prompt injection.

GERMANY: AI SEARCH AND CHATBOTS UNDER MEDIA LAW (July 16)
Germany's Commission for Licensing and Supervision (ZAK) issued its first
rulings applying the State Media Treaty to AI services, targeting Google's AI
Overviews and Perplexity:

  - AI-generated summaries classified as providers' own content, not third-party
  - Digital Services Act liability exemptions do not apply
  - Google accused of discriminating against journalistic links by placing AI
    summaries prominently above traditional search results
  - Both companies have one month to appeal; rulings are immediately enforceable

ZAK Chairman Dr. Thorsten Schmiege: "AI search engines and chatbots are content
providers — and we are now consistently applying German media law to them."

CHINA: AI COMPANION REGULATIONS TAKE EFFECT (July 15)
China's Interim Measures for the Administration of AI Anthropomorphic
Interactive Services took effect, the first major jurisdiction to regulate
emotionally interactive AI companions:

  - Targets services offering sustained emotional interaction via text, audio,
    or video; excludes customer service, work assistants, and education tools
  - Mandatory two-hour usage reminders, anti-addiction systems, crisis
    intervention for emotional distress
  - Virtual intimate relationships prohibited for users under 18
  - Users gain rights to copy and delete interaction data

ByteDance's Doubao, Alibaba's Qwen, and Tencent's Yuanbao suspended custom AI
agent and companion features ahead of the deadline. Users reported losing access
to personalized chat histories, prompting emotional farewells on social media.

EU: GOOGLE MUST OPEN ANDROID AND SEARCH TO RIVALS (July 16)
The European Commission issued two binding decisions under the Digital Markets Act:

  - Google must give rival AI assistants the same system-level access as Gemini
    on Android (voice activation, app interaction, hardware access)
  - Google must share anonymized search data with third-party search engines and
    AI chatbots, using the same data it uses to optimize its own search
  - Deadline: search data sharing by January 2027; Android changes by July 2027

Google's Kent Walker criticized the decisions as undermining privacy and security
guardrails. Apple also moved to Google's defense on security concerns.
